# D&D 2024 channels (SRD 5.2)

A channel plays by one rule system, chosen by its GM: **Call of Cthulhu 7th edition** (BRP-based, the default), [PbtA](PBTA.md), or
**D&D 2024** from the System Reference Document 5.2. These are channel options, not server options:

- GM workspace → Settings → **Rules** → *Rule system* (`PUT /channels/{id}` `{rule_system: coc7e|dnd2024|pbta}`), and
- *Point buy for role cards* (`{point_buy: true}`), which applies the point buy of the channel's rule system.

The sheet, the dice commands and the AI prompts follow the new system at once. Every character keeps its story and its
numbers are rebuilt; see [Switching the rule system](#switching-the-rule-system).
Point buy used to be the server feature `coc7_points`; migration 0042 gave each channel its server's old value.

## What changes in a D&D channel

| | Call of Cthulhu 7e | D&D 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Character sheet | Investigator card (characteristics, skills %, SAN…) | D&D sheet (class, species, background, ability scores, proficiencies, HP, slots) |
| Checks | `.ra`/`.rc` percentile with hard/extreme, bonus/penalty dice | `.rc`/`.ra` D20 Tests with Advantage/Disadvantage, DC/AC |
| Other commands | `.sc`, `.en` | `.ri`/`.rih`, `.init`, `.ds`, `.ss` |
| Shared | `.r`, `.rh`, `.st` | `.r`, `.rh`, `.st` |
| AI prompts | "Call of Cthulhu / BRP", "Keeper", "KP" | "a fifth-edition fantasy roleplaying game (D&D 2024 rules, SRD 5.2)", "Dungeon Master", "DM" |
| Module import | NPCs, locations, clues… | also `creature` nodes with stat blocks |

A command of the other system answers with a short error ("… uses the D&D rules" / "… uses the Call of Cthulhu
rules") instead of rolling.

## Dice commands

Ability, skill and save names are understood in all eleven server languages ("stealth", "隐匿", "Heimlichkeit"; "dex
save", "敏捷豁免"), and the reply uses the channel's dice text language.

- `.rc <ability | skill | <ability> save | attack> [+N | -N | +1d4] [adv | dis] [dc N | ac N] [#reason]`
  A D20 Test. The bonus comes from the sheet: the ability modifier, plus the Proficiency Bonus when the skill or save is
  proficient (twice it with Expertise). `adv`/`dis` roll two d20s and keep the higher/lower (both together cancel).
  `dc 15` reports success or failure; `ac 13` makes it an attack roll against that Armor Class. Following the 2024
  rules, a natural 20 or 1 decides only attack rolls (a Critical Hit, or a miss); ability checks and saving throws just
  add the bonus. `.ra` is the same command; `.rch`/`.rah` roll in secret.
- `.ri [+N | -N] [adv | dis] [name]` rolls Initiative (DEX modifier plus the sheet's Initiative bonus) and puts the
  speaker into the window's Initiative order. With a name (`.ri +2 Goblin`) the roll is for that name, with the typed
  bonus only; GMs use it for monsters. Rolling again replaces the earlier entry of the same name. `.rih` is hidden.
- `.init` shows the order; `.init next` (GM) moves to the next turn and starts a new round after the last;
  `.init del <name>` removes an entry (players: their own); `.init clr` (GM) clears the order.
- `.ds` rolls a Death Saving Throw: 10 or higher is a success, lower a failure, a 1 counts as two failures and a 20
  brings the character back to 1 Hit Point. Three successes stabilise (the counts reset), three failures mean death.
- `.ss` lists spell slots; `.ss 2` spends a level 2 slot, `.ss 2 -2` two of them, `.ss 2 +1` regains one, and
  `.ss reset` restores all of them (a Long Rest).
- `.st` sets or changes sheet values as in a CoC channel: `.st hp-7`, `.st str16 dex14`, `.st show`.

## The character sheet

The sheet builds a character from the SRD 5.2 options: 12 classes (hit die, saving throw proficiencies, skill list and
count, spellcasting: full, half or Pact Magic), 9 species (speed, the skill trait of Elves and Humans) and the four SRD
backgrounds (three abilities for the increases, two skills). Class, species and background names are shown in English,
Simplified and Traditional Chinese; other languages show the English names.

- **Ability scores**: point buy (27 points, scores 8–15 before increases, costs 0/1/2/3/4/5/7/9), the standard array
  (15, 14, 13, 12, 10, 8) or rolled/entered values; then the background's increases (+2 and +1, or +1 to each of its
  three abilities, at most 20), then later improvements (Ability Score Improvements and feats).
- **Proficiencies**: saving throws from the class; skills from the class (choose the count), the background (its two)
  and the species; Expertise doubles the bonus.
- **Combat**: Armor Class, Speed, Initiative bonus, Hit Point maximum (the sheet suggests the hit die maximum at level 1
  and its average after that, each plus the CON modifier), Hit Dice; live Hit Points, Temporary Hit Points and Death
  Saving Throws.
- **Spellcasting**: spellcasting ability, spell save DC and attack bonus, slots per Long Rest filled from the class and
  level, slots left; features, feats, spells and notes as text.

The build is stored in `role_card.dnd`. Saving it mirrors the scores, Level, Proficiency Bonus, proficiencies (`Stealth`
0/1/2, `DEX Save` 0/1), AC, Speed, Initiative, HP Max and `Slot N Max` (from the class table for its level when a caster's card names no
slots) into the sheet attributes the dice read. The
values play changes (HP, Temp HP, `Slot N`, death saves) are only filled in when missing, so saving the card never undoes
what happened at the table.

### Point buy

With *Point buy for role cards* on, the server checks every saved D&D card and refuses one that breaks a rule
(`400 points_rule`, with the reason): the method must be point buy or the standard array; the 27-point budget and the
8–15 range; the background's increases (only its three abilities, +2/+1 or +1/+1/+1, at most 20); the class's saving
throws; the number of class skills and that they come from the class list (background and species skills come on top).
Later improvements are the GM's to grant: a player's save keeps the stored improvements. The GM is held to the same
build rules.

## Initiative panel

In a D&D channel the chat window shows the Initiative order above the messages: entries highest first (ties by DEX
modifier), the current turn and round, a *Start / Next turn* and a *Clear* button for the GM, and remove buttons on
entries the viewer may remove. It updates live (`initiative.changed` on `/ws/chat`). Each chat window has its own order;
it lives in `initiative_entries` and `chat_windows.initiative_turn` / `initiative_round`.

## AI prompts

The prompts are written for Call of Cthulhu. For a D&D channel the AI gateway rewrites the system prompt once before the
call: the game is named as above, "Keeper" becomes "Dungeon Master" and "KP" "DM"; the consistency check gets D&D rule
hints (the DM adjudicates, characters know only what they saw or were told, rolls follow the D20 Test rules); visual
novel scripts label the GM "DM". Chat lines, documents and other data are never rewritten, and Call of Cthulhu channels
get exactly the prompts they had before, so their prompt caches stay valid.

## Creature stat blocks in module import

In a D&D channel the module import asks for `creature` nodes for monsters and other combatants and copies their stat
block: size and type, AC, HP and its formula, Speed, CR, the six ability scores, saving throws, skills, senses,
languages, and traits, actions, bonus actions, reactions and legendary actions (`{name, text}` each). The module panel
shows it as a stat block under the creature. Imports in CoC channels are unchanged.

## Switching the rule system

A character card has two parts. The **story part** belongs to the character whatever the rules: the name, bio and
speaking style, the basic information (player, occupation, age, sex, residence, birthplace), the background, the items,
and the look (appearance prompt, avatar, portrait and subtitle images). The **number part** belongs to one rule system:
Call of Cthulhu's characteristics, derived values, skills, career and skill points and wealth, D&D's build
(`role_card.dnd`: class, species, background, level, scores, proficiencies, AC, HP, slots, features and spells), or
PbtA's playbook (`role_card.pbta`: book, stats, resources, conditions), plus the dice values in `sheet.attrs` that `.st`
and the dice read.

When the GM switches the rules (`PUT /channels/{id}` with another `rule_system`), in the same transaction:

1. **The story part stays** on every card, untouched.
2. **The old numbers are archived read-only** (`character_card_archives`: the number part and the dice values, with the
   system, the time and who switched). The character space lists them under *Former cards*, drawn with the old system's
   sheet; there is no way to edit them.
3. **The card gets the new system's blank numbers**, and its dice values are cleared, so nothing of the old system is
   rolled by mistake. A save from a client still holding the old numbers (an open editor, an older app) cannot put them
   back: a card only ever keeps the channel's own system's numbers.
4. **The players are asked to rebuild.** Every character that had numbers is flagged (`characters.card_pending`): the
   character list shows *rebuild* next to it, its card opens with a notice, and a system line in the main window names
   the PCs to rebuild (NPCs are never named to players). The response carries
   `rule_transition {from, to, archived, pending, rebuild[{id, name}]}`, and the GM's confirmation and toast say the same.

The flag goes once the numbers are rebuilt: for D&D when the card has a class, for PbtA when it has a playbook, for
Call of Cthulhu when it has the eight characteristics (Luck aside). The GM may also waive it (*Mark as rebuilt*, `card_rebuilt: true` on the card save),
for an NPC that will never roll or a card kept elsewhere; a player cannot.

**Going back.** Switching back archives the numbers built meanwhile in the same way. A former card of the channel's
current system offers *Use this card again* (`POST /channels/{id}/characters/{cid}/card-archives/{archive}/restore`,
the GM or the character's player): its numbers and dice values return to the card, whatever was on the card is archived
in turn (reason `restore`), the archive entry is used up, and the flag goes. A former card of another system cannot be
restored (409), nor a PbtA card that no longer fits the channel's rule pack (409: rebuild it). A character without numbers (a blank card, or only blank-card defaults) is neither archived nor flagged.

Coming back to a system does not bring its numbers back by itself (an earlier version parked the dice values per
system in `sheet.rule_attrs` and kept every system's numbers on the card): the card is new for the system, and the
former one comes back only when its player or the GM chooses *Use this card again*.

Channels that switched before this existed were cleaned up by migration 0050: numbers of another system still on a card
were archived (reason `legacy`) with the dice values parked for that system in `sheet.rule_attrs`, which is gone since,
and the character was flagged unless its card already has the current system's numbers.
